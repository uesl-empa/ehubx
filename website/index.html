Find the best energy system for your district, municipality or region.

ehubX is a planning tool that works out which energy technologies to build, how large they should be, and how to run them. It weighs costs, CO₂ emissions and local self-sufficiency against each other, so decision-makers can compare options on solid numbers.

What is ehubX?

A “what-if” engine for energy planning

Planning a local energy system means making many connected decisions. Should we install heat pumps or connect to a district heating network? How much solar is worth it? Do we need a battery, or seasonal heat storage? What happens when energy prices or CO₂ targets change?

ehubX answers these questions together, not one at a time. You describe the area: its energy demand, the technologies available, prices and local limits. ehubX then uses mathematical optimisation to find the combination that best meets your goal, for every hour of the year and, if you want, over several decades of investment.

Questions ehubX helps answer

  • €What is the cheapest way to meet our energy needs?The mix and size of technologies with the lowest total cost over their lifetime.
  • CO₂What does it cost to cut emissions further?The trade-off between costs and emissions, so you can see how much each extra tonne of CO₂ saved costs.
  • ⌂How self-sufficient can we become?How much local energy can be produced and used, and how long the area could keep running without outside supply.
  • ⇢When should we invest?A step-by-step investment plan, for example 2030, 2040 and 2050, that follows changing prices, demand and targets.
  • ⇄Should neighbouring sites share energy?Whether heat or power networks between buildings, sites or towns pay off, and where they should run.
  • ↻How should the system run day to day?When to charge storage, run heat pumps or shift flexible loads, hour by hour.

How it works

From a description of your area to a clear recommendation

  1. 1

    Describe the system

    Energy demand profiles, prices, available technologies and their costs, and limits such as roof area or grid connection size. All inputs are plain spreadsheet-style files.

  2. 2

    Set a goal

    Minimise cost, emissions, or dependence on imports. Or explore the trade-off between two goals at once.

  3. 3

    Optimise

    ehubX sets up a mathematical model and solves it with proven industrial solvers, taking every hour of the year into account.

  4. 4

    Get answers

    Which technologies to build and how large, how they run every hour, and the resulting costs, emissions and self-sufficiency.

The “energy hub” idea

ehubX treats each building, site or town as an energy hub, a node where different forms of energy come together. Electricity, heat, gas, hydrogen and other carriers can be bought, produced, stored, converted into each other and passed on to neighbouring hubs. At every hour, what comes in must equal what goes out.

Looking at all energy forms together is called sector coupling. It shows combinations that stay hidden when electricity, heating and mobility are planned separately. One example is solar power running heat pumps whose heat is stored for winter.

What can be modelled

A broad toolbox of technologies

ehubX is a framework, not one fixed model. Each study combines the building blocks it needs.

  • Solar & windRooftop and ground-mounted PV, solar thermal, wind turbines
  • Heat pumpsWith temperature-dependent efficiency
  • ConversionBoilers, CHP, electrolysers and any carrier-to-carrier technology
  • StorageBatteries, hot-water tanks, hydrogen and more
  • Seasonal heat storageAquifer thermal energy storage (ATES)
  • Electric mobilityEV charging and vehicle batteries
  • Energy networksHeat and power grids between hubs, including losses
  • Flexible demandLoad shifting and load shedding
  • MarketsEnergy imports and exports with time-varying prices
  • Long-term planningMulti-stage investment over decades
  • Multiple goalsCost, CO₂, self-sufficiency and autonomy
  • Fast studiesClustering typical days to speed up large models

Use cases

Who uses ehubX, and for what

Municipal & regional energy planning

Compare strategies to reach net zero across a whole town or region, including costs, emissions and the infrastructure needed, as a basis for energy plans and political decisions.

Industrial sites & sector coupling

Find synergies between industry, households and energy suppliers, such as using industrial waste heat, electrifying processes or capturing CO₂.

District heating & seasonal storage

Size heat networks, heat pumps and underground seasonal storage so that summer heat can be used in winter.

Neighbourhoods & building portfolios

Choose between individual and shared solutions for groups of buildings, such as PV, batteries, heat pumps or a local heat network.

Resilience & autonomy

Work out how long a community or site could keep supplying itself during a grid outage or supply crisis, and what that would cost.

Research

A transparent, open-source basis for scientific studies on energy transition pathways, new technologies and flexibility.

      Get started

      Install

      ehubX is a Python package (3.11+) available on PyPI. You also need a MILP solver: Gurobi or the free GLPK.

      pip install ehubx

      The documentation covers input files, all parameters and the full mathematical model.
